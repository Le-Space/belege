# @le-space/chains

Own wallets on public chains, read only, by their address – for [Belege](https://github.com/Le-Space/belege) (its bridge) and the [Eigenbeleg](https://github.com/Le-Space/eigenbeleg-agent) API and web UI. Every reader hands out [`@le-space/ledger`](../ledger) entries: one per movement of value, signed from the wallet's side, the fee as an entry of its own.

Runs in a browser and in Node. The caller hands in `fetch`, the endpoints (a node, an indexer, a proxy) and the keys (an Alchemy key, a NearBlocks key, a Bitcoin zpub/xpub); nothing is read from disk, a keychain or the environment. A browser can read the keyless chains itself where the source allows CORS (or behind a proxy).

- `createWalletService({ fetch, getZpub, alchemyKey, … })` – every chain behind one `history({ chain, address, endpoints })`, as Belege's bridge serves it.
- EVM tokens not in the list (#115, #93): one the address sent on itself, or whose contract the caller names (`tokens: ['0x…']` to `evmReader().read`/`statement` and `history`), is booked by its contract (`contract`, `listed: false`, no `priceAs`), its symbol the token's own claim, and its balance is read with the others: a statement gets a section for it after the native coin and the listed tokens, ordered by contract, and names its contract in `contracts` (asset → contract), also for one without a booking in the period. Never under a symbol a listed asset or a big token has (`USDC`, `ETH`, …); an airdrop never touched stays out.
- `CHAINS`, `chainOf`, `publicChains` – the chains, their assets, default endpoints and explorers (`registry`). One chain's data without the others: `FILECOIN` (`filecoin-chain`), `NYX_CHAIN`, `AKASH_CHAIN` (`cosmos-chains`), `KNOWN_EVM_CONTRACTS` (`evm-contracts`). The readers take their chain as an argument and do not import the registry.
- The `LedgerEntry` readers (`near`, `akash`, `filecoin`, `evmReader`, `aleph`, `doichain`, `bitcoin-address`) each give `read…`/`statement…` for a period (`statement`: the entries and the balances before and after, `method: 'archive'` on NEAR, `'derived'` from today's balance elsewhere), `probe` as a live check against public data, `isAddress`, and for Aleph `purchaseEntries`: the payment behind each credit purchase. From the Eigenbeleg agent's pipeline readers; its blocks are built on them.
- `createFilecoinPayClient().statement({ address, month })` (`filecoin-pay`) – what a payer's balance in Filecoin Pay was used for in a month: opening and closing balance, paid in, taken out, and each settlement of each rail (provider, service, amount, of it commission and network fee, paid up to when), valued in euros. Storage there is paid by the contract out of that balance, not by transfers of a wallet, so no wallet reader sees it. Read from the makers' index first and from the contract's events at Blockscout when the index does not answer (`endpoints.blockscoutKey` for a key of dev.blockscout.com, sent to `api.blockscout.com` in the `authorization` header and in no URL – the client's `keyed` option names that host, so a caller of `statement` cannot have the key sent elsewhere; without a key Blockscout allows about ten requests, then none for minutes). `source` and `movementsFrom` on the statement say which way it came. `filecoinPayEntries` gives its lines as `LedgerEntry`. Runs in Node and in a browser.
- `toChecksumAddress`, `isEvmAddress` – an EVM address's EIP-55 checksum and form (`evm-address`: keccak and bytes only, so `aleph` and a browser form do not bundle the EVM reader or Alchemy; `evm` re-exports them).
- `createJsonFetcher`, `WalletError`, `checkEndpoint`, `txUrl`, `addressUrl`, `markSwaps` – what every reader shares (`core`). Error messages name a host at most, never a path or a query (they carry addresses).

Each source sees the address (an ElectrumX server its script hash) and the IP address of the machine that asks; Belege's consent screen says so.
