# Crypto assets: quantities, rates, bookings

_Deutsch: [crypto.de.md](crypto.de.md)_

Belege keeps accounts on exchanges (Kraken) and own wallets on blockchains (Cosmos, EVM, Bitcoin, Filecoin, NEAR; Monero by import). This page describes what they share, the Kraken connector and the wallets.

## One account per asset

A crypto account holds exactly one asset: _Kraken BTC_, _Kraken EUR_ and _Wallet NYM_ are three accounts, each with its own ledger account in the bookkeeping. The assets Belege knows are listed in `app/src/lib/assets/registry.js`: symbol, name, decimals and, where it is certain, the chain-agnostic CAIP-19 id.

## A crypto transaction is booked in euros and keeps what moved

Belege books in EUR cents, as it does for the bank accounts: `amountCents` is what the movement was worth on its day. Next to it, the transaction keeps:

| Field       | Example                                                                               | Meaning                                                                                                                            |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `asset`     | `BTC`                                                                                 | the symbol                                                                                                                         |
| `quantity`  | `"-1500000"`                                                                          | the signed amount in the smallest unit (here satoshi: −0.015 BTC), as a string, because 18-decimal tokens do not fit into a double |
| `decimals`  | `8`                                                                                   | the asset's decimals, so the record explains itself                                                                                |
| `movement`  | `transfer`                                                                            | `transfer`, `trade`, `fee`, `reward` or `stake`                                                                                    |
| `txRef`     | `…`                                                                                   | the transaction hash or the exchange's reference                                                                                   |
| `valuation` | `{ rate: "60000", currency: "EUR", source: "coingecko", at: "2026-09-01T00:00:00Z" }` | EUR per whole unit, where the rate came from and for which moment                                                                  |

`quantity` × `rate` gives `amountCents`, computed in integers and rounded once, half away from zero (`app/src/lib/assets/quantity.js`). Nothing about a crypto amount goes through a floating-point number.

## The rate of a day

The bridge answers `GET /rates?asset=BTC&date=2026-09-01` (`packages/rates/src/rates.js`). The browser cannot ask the sources itself (CORS), and an optional CoinGecko key stays in the keychain. The rate is always the value of one unit **at 00:00 UTC of that day**:

1. **CoinGecko**, its daily snapshot (`/coins/{id}/history`);
2. failing that, **Kraken**, the open price of the day's daily candle. CoinGecko answers for the last 365 days only, and Kraken's daily candles cover the last 720. For an older day the bridge asks Kraken's trade history for the first trade of that day after 00:00 UTC: the price the candle would have opened with. A day before Kraken listed the pair has no rate;

   for a booking **on Kraken** (`prefer=kraken`) the order turns: Kraken's own EUR price first, CoinGecko as the fallback. Kraken then also prices assets Belege does not list yet, by their `<SYMBOL>EUR` pair;

3. for **USD** and the other currencies the ECB publishes: the **ECB reference rate** of the day, or the last one before it (weekends, holidays), inverted to EUR per unit; for **RUB** after 1 March 2022, when the ECB stopped publishing it, the **Bank of Russia's** official rate valid on the day; for **KZT** the **National Bank of Kazakhstan's** official rate of the day; for a currency neither the ECB nor these publish against the euro but the Bank of Russia rates (UZS, GEL, AMD, KGS, AED, …) a **cross rate through the ruble** from the Bank of Russia's file of the day – marked as such (`cbr-cross`, CBR-X), not an official euro rate (#325);
4. for a **token not in the list** on Ethereum or Base, when CoinGecko does not know its contract: its **Uniswap pool** at the booking's block (Uniswap V2, V3 and V4, against ETH or USDC), the deepest one and only if it holds at least 3,000 EUR. This needs an Alchemy key ([Alchemy](#alchemy)); without one there is no pool rate (#163, `packages/rates/src/dex-rate.js`).

The request names an asset and a day, nothing about the bookings. Past days are cached in memory. With a CoinGecko demo key in the keychain (account `coingecko`), it is sent as a header.

**Which rate and which valuation method apply is the tax adviser's call.** This includes the accounts for crypto holdings, gains and losses on exchange rates, FIFO or average cost, and valuation at the end of the year. Belege records the rate and its source on every booking, so a different decision can be applied later.

## Kraken

**Set up** (once): on kraken.com create an API key with only **Query Funds** and **Query Ledger Entries**. Then run `pnpm setup:kraken` and paste the API key and the private key into the hidden prompts. If `KRAKEN_API_KEY` and `KRAKEN_PRIVATE_KEY` are in `.env`, the setup offers to take them from there. The key goes into the macOS keychain (account `kraken`), and a test call prints how many assets have a balance, never an amount. Restart the bridge. An optional CoinGecko demo key is set up the same way with `pnpm setup:coingecko` (`COINGECKO_API_KEY`).

**Sync**: _Integrationen → Kraken (Börse) → Kraken synchronisieren_. The bridge reads the ledger (`GET /kraken/ledgers`, paged by `ofs`, 50 entries a page, with a pause and retries on Kraken's rate limit) and the balances (`GET /kraken/balances`). The first sync starts on 1 January; later ones start a week before the last.

**Under the hood**: the requests go through [ccxt](https://github.com/ccxt/ccxt) (MIT), which signs them and maps Kraken's error codes; the bridge uses its raw endpoints, so the entries are Kraken's own and are read as before. The nonce stays in microseconds, as the bridge has always sent it – Kraken keeps the highest nonce a key has sent and refuses a lower one. More exchanges through ccxt are planned (#265).

**Accounts**: one per asset and wallet: _Kraken EUR_, _Kraken BTC_, _Kraken BTC (Earn)_ for staked or earning balances (Kraken's `.S`, `.M`, … assets). Each needs its ledger account in the bookkeeping like a bank account (_Einstellungen → Buchhaltung (DATEV)_).

**Bookings**: one per ledger entry, plus one for each fee Kraken charged on it (so the fee is visible and booked on 4970).

| Entry                                                | Euro amount                                                                     | Recognised as                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| EUR deposit / withdrawal                             | the amount                                                                      | own transfer with the bank booking of the same amount within 4 days (sign: _Kraken_)                                                                     |
| buy or sell against EUR (`trade`, `spend`/`receive`) | the crypto leg is worth exactly what was paid or received (rate source `trade`) | own transfer between the two Kraken accounts (same reference)                                                                                            |
| crypto against crypto                                | the outgoing leg at the day's rate, the incoming leg mirrors it                 | own transfer (same reference)                                                                                                                            |
| spot ↔ earn                                         | the day's rate, both legs                                                       | own transfer (same reference)                                                                                                                            |
| staking / earn reward                                | the day's rate                                                                  | _Ertrag der Börse_: no receipt needed; suggested on 2700 Sonstige Erträge (or the account set in the settings), for the tax adviser to confirm           |
| fee                                                  | at the rate of its entry                                                        | exchange fee, 4970                                                                                                                                       |
| crypto deposit / withdrawal                          | the day's rate                                                                  | own transfer with the wallet booking that has the same transaction hash (Kraken's `txid` from DepositStatus / WithdrawStatus), whatever the euro amounts |

**Labels** follow Kraken's type and subtype: only `spottostaking`, `stakingfromspot` and their like are _Umbuchung Spot/Earn_, `spottofutures`/`spotfromfutures` _Umbuchung Spot/Futures_. A subtype Belege does not know is shown as Kraken writes it (`Kraken: transfer/…`), never guessed. The payment detail shows Kraken's type, the reference and the transaction hash, and lists the other bookings with the same reference (_Gehört zusammen mit_).

Two sides paired by reference but valued apart (a Kraken withdrawal and the wallet that received it, on different days) are exported each against 1360, so the difference in value stays visible there.

An entry whose rate cannot be found is left out with the rest of its trade, listed after the sync, and fetched again next time.

**Not decided here**: gains and losses when crypto is sold (acquisition cost by FIFO or average) and the year-end valuation. Every booking keeps quantity, rate and source, so the tax adviser's method can be applied to it.

### Which chain a deposit's hash is on (issue #215)

A deposit or withdrawal keeps Kraken's on-chain hash (`chainTxRef`) and, where Kraken names it, the network (`chainMethod`, e.g. `Filecoin`, `Ether (Arbitrum One)`). The payment's detail tells the chain from these, locally, in this order: an own wallet's booking with the same hash; Kraken's network name, where it fits the hash; the hash's form with the asset – a CID `bafy2bzace…` is Filecoin, `0x` + 64 hex an EVM chain (the asset's home chain where it has one, POL → Polygon), 64 hex Bitcoin for BTC (Nyx for NYM, Akash for AKT), base58 of 87–88 characters Solana. A clear chain gets its name and a link to its explorer (Filfox, mempool.space, Etherscan and its siblings, Nodes Guru, Mintscan, Solscan); an EVM hash with no network named lists the EVM chains it can be on, each linked. No explorer is asked: a link opens on your click (`app/src/lib/assets/hash-chain.js`).

A crypto withdrawal also keeps the address it went to (`chainAddress`, Kraken's `info` in WithdrawStatus; a deposit's `info` is Kraken's own address and is not kept). When that address is no own wallet yet, on a chain Belege reads by address, the payment's detail says so and offers _Als eigene Wallet anlegen_: the click opens _Integrationen → Eigene Wallets_ with the chain and the address filled in. Nothing is added or asked of any node before you press the button there – an address is no proof of ownership, and a withdrawal can pay someone else. With an EVM hash of no named network, every EVM chain is offered. For a chain Belege cannot read (Solana, say) the detail says that the arrival will not be found. Bitcoin (read by a key in the bridge) and Monero (imported) are not offered (`app/src/lib/wallets/suggest.js`).

The same detail asks **what the transfer was** (_Was war diese Zahlung?_), for an exchange's withdrawal and for a wallet's own booking to or from an address that nothing else explains. The answer is kept for the address, on the partner record, so every booking with it is treated the same:

- _an own wallet_ – added and read, as above; the two sides become an own transfer;
- _private_ – the person's own private address, a withdrawal or a deposit: no receipt, counted as private;
- _a vendor or service provider_ (incoming: _a customer_) – someone else's address: the address gets the name, and the payment needs its invoice;
- _own money at an exchange or custodian_ Belege does not read: no receipt, an own transfer on 1360 whose other side is not in the books, so 1360 stays open by that amount.

What the books can see for themselves – an own wallet, a counter-booking, the same hash – always comes first. An answer can be taken back (_Aufheben_). How a withdrawal, a swap or staking is to be booked for tax is for the tax adviser; Belege only proposes (`app/src/lib/matching/partners.js` `ADDRESS_KINDS`, `classify.js`).

## Own wallets

Tokens withdrawn from an exchange to a wallet of our own stay the company's. _Integrationen → Eigene Wallets_ adds such a wallet by **chain and address**: read only, never a key or a seed phrase. The list of wallets is kept sealed in the books (settings key `wallets`); the bridge keeps nothing about them.

**The page** has three tabs; the one chosen last is kept by the browser (localStorage `belege.walletsTab`, a UI preference, no data of the books). _Wallets_ lists the wallets grouped by chain, each group in the colour its chain has in the value chart (the four chains worth most get a colour of their own, the rest grey): chains with a failed sync first, then those whose bookings miss the balance, then by value and by name. A group folds (on a phone they start folded), names how many wallets it has, and its _Kette synchronisieren_ syncs only that chain's wallets, as _Alle … synchronisieren_ does all. A search field above finds a wallet by name, address, chain or asset. Each wallet is one row: a status icon, the name and the address (click to copy), its balances right-aligned and rounded to at most four decimals – below 1 to four significant digits, below 0.0001 as "< 0,0001"; the exact figure is in the tooltip and the details –, when it was last synced, a sync icon, a ⋯ menu (name, ledger account and cost centre · the block explorer · remove) and the details: the full address, the endpoints with the service each is (Blockscout for EVM chains, Esplora at mempool.space for Bitcoin, Filfox for Filecoin, NearBlocks for NEAR), the ledger account and cost centre, each asset account with its exact balance and the balance check, the last sync and a failed sync's full message with its way out. A sync's error is said once per group, with the wallets it is for. _Übersicht_ has the value chart and, per chain, the wallets, what they hold and what the chain was worth on the last day with a rate. _Nachweise_ has the monthly statements: Akash's per Akash wallet, Filecoin Pay's, and a link to the Aleph statements, which belong to the Aleph integration (#1019).

**Chains**: Nym (Nyx) and Akash (Cosmos SDK), Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum One, OP Mainnet and Polygon PoS (EVM), [Bitcoin](#bitcoin), [Filecoin](#filecoin), [NEAR](#near), and [Monero](#monero) by importing the wallet's export. The table is `packages/chains/src/registry.js`; the app's names for them are in `app/src/lib/wallets/chains.js`.

**Sync** (_Synchronisieren_, the icon on a wallet's row): the app sends the address in the body of `POST /<chain>/wallet` to the bridge, and the bridge asks a public node (see below; a wallet may name its own https endpoint instead). It reads the whole history each time; what is known is skipped.

**All wallets at once** (_Alle … Wallets synchronisieren_): the wallets synced by their address, one after the other, with the progress in percent on the card and on the tab _Integrationen_. It goes on while you click elsewhere in Belege; only a reload stops it, and what was synced stays. At the end the card says what came: new bookings, bookings without a rate, wallets that could not be fetched. If Belege is in the background then and new bookings came, a system notification says so – with counts only, never an amount, a name or an address, since it may show on a locked screen. The browser asks for that permission once, on the first click on the button. A Monero wallet is not part of it: its history is a file you import.

- **Cosmos**: CometBFT RPC `tx_search` for `transfer.sender='<address>'` and `transfer.recipient='<address>'` (a send, a fee, a reward are `transfer` events; a delegation is not – the bank keeper's `DelegateCoins` emits only `coin_spent` and `coin_received`, so such a pair without a following `transfer` is read as a movement too, and on chains before cosmos-sdk 0.44 the `delegate` event; the delegation pays its fee with a transfer, so the sender query finds it; issue #135), 100 a page, oldest first, merged by hash; `header` for each block's time; `status` for the oldest block the node still has; REST `/cosmos/bank/v1beta1/balances/<address>` for the balance; the memo from the transaction's bytes.
- **Akash, older history** (issue #105): every public Akash node is pruned. When the node's lowest block is above 1, the bridge reads what lies before it from the Akash Console indexer (`console-api.akash.network`, run by the Akash team, open source, no key): the address's transactions (`/v1/addresses/<address>/transactions/<skip>/<limit>`), then each one by hash (`/v1/transactions/<hash>`) for its decoded messages, signers and fee. From the messages it books sends, multi-sends, IBC transfers out and back home, delegations, escrow deposits from the balance and governance deposits, plus the fee of every transaction the address signed first (`packages/chains/src/akash-console.js`). The indexer has no events, so it has no amount that only an event carries: withdrawn rewards, the rewards a (un-/re-)delegation withdraws on the way, escrow refunds, lease payouts and authz `MsgExec`. Those transactions are counted and shown on the wallet card, and the balance check shows the gap – unless the wallet names an **archive node** (#1029): a CometBFT RPC that keeps every block, in the wallet's field _Archivknoten_ (`endpoints.archive`). No public Akash node keeps every block (checked 2026-11-25: every RPC and REST in the chain registry, Lavender Five, PublicNode, Akash's own – the lowest height between 26.9 and 29.7 million), so it is the person's own; nothing is asked of it by default, and the consent screen's "the node you name" covers it. For each transaction the indexer has no amount for, the bridge reads its block from that node – `block` for the transactions' bytes (the SHA-256 of which is the hash) and `block_results` for their events, so no transaction index is needed – and books it exactly as tx_search would: the rewards (`reward`, income like any staking reward, #1027), escrow refunds, the rewards an undelegation withdraws, in place of what the indexer read of the same transaction (the delegation the indexer booked from its message keeps its id through `reconcileSourceIds`). Only figures the chain gives; a block the node cannot answer stays counted. If the node's window moves on, a transaction first read from the node is read from the indexer later under another id; the app keeps the id it was booked under (`reconcileSourceIds`: same hash, kind and quantity). Checked on 2026-09-27: history back to March 2021 (mainnet-2); fees and sends agree with the node's events; reward amounts are 0 in the indexer.
- **Unbonding returns** (#1029): the tokens an undelegation gives back are no transaction. The chain pays them out at the end of the first block at or after the undelegation's completion time, with a `complete_unbonding` event (delegator, validator, amount). The bridge takes the completion time from the undelegation's `unbond` event, finds that block by its time (a binary search over block headers) and books the event's amount as a movement `stake` received from `not_bonded_tokens_pool`, without a hash: id `unbonding:<height>:<validator>:<asset>`, the purpose names the block. All of a validator's unbondings that end in the same block are one event and one booking. For an undelegation only the Akash indexer knows (no events, no completion time), the block is looked for at its time plus the chain's unbonding period (`/cosmos/staking/v1beta1/params`); only an event found there is booked. The block comes from the archive node if there is one, else from the node, whose window may still hold it. An unbonding still running is not a gap; one whose block no node can give is counted on the wallet card. This holds for every Cosmos chain (Nyx too).
- **Akash burn-mint (BME, #303):** deployments are paid in ACT, which `MsgMintACT` mints by burning AKT. Such a transaction is a swap AKT → ACT (_Tausch … über Akash BME_), with no receipt needed, like a DEX swap. From the node, both sides come from the events (ACT is named, not listed). From the indexer, the AKT burnt comes from the message; the ACT minted is in the events only, so its amount is "unknown". `MsgBurnACT` (ACT back to AKT) is counted from the indexer, as its AKT is in the events only.
- **A Kraken withdrawal the listing leaves out (#303):** the indexer's listing for an address can miss a transaction, for example the wallet's first funding from Kraken. On a sync, the app hands the bridge the on-chain hashes of its Kraken withdrawals in the chain's native asset that no wallet booking has yet. The bridge asks the indexer for each by hash and books the ones that move something of this wallet's, as an own transfer paired with the withdrawal. Only those hashes leave the app, to the indexer it already asks.
- **Monthly usage statement (#305):** Akash has no supplier who invoices. In the tab _Nachweise_, on an Akash wallet's card, _Verbrauchsnachweis erstellen_ makes an Eigenbeleg per month (EB-YYYY-NNN, as Aleph's). It lists:
  - the network fees with their hashes;
  - AKT burnt for ACT;
  - each deployment's usage. The node lists an owner's deployments with their escrow (`/akash/deployment/v1beta4/deployments/list`), and the indexer gives the blocks' times. The chain keeps only each deployment's total paid to its providers, so that total is spread evenly over the deployment's time and the month's share is counted. ACT is valued at 1 USD (Akash's burn-mint) at the ECB rate of the month's last day.

  The statement becomes a receipt linked to the month's network fees. In the DATEV export, those fees go in as one collective booking per wallet account (switchable on the export page). The valuation is to be confirmed with the tax adviser.

- **ACT account (#305):** _ACT-Konto anzeigen_ on an Akash wallet's card shows Akash as a vendor account, month by month: ACT topped up (burn-mint), used by the deployments, and the balance. What adds up: minted = held now (the node's `uact` balance) + left in escrow + used. An older mint's ACT is no longer on chain; the ACT those mints gave together then follows from that identity and is shared by the AKT each burnt. In euros, at cost: a top-up costs what its AKT was booked at, each ACT the average of all top-ups. Usage and what is left are valued at that cost. Whether a top-up is a prepayment and the usage the expense is for the tax adviser.

- **EVM**: first Blockscout's JSON-RPC proxy (`…/api/eth-rpc`, `eth_chainId`) must name the chain (else `WALLET_WRONG_CHAIN`, or `WALLET_CHAIN_UNVERIFIED` when it cannot say); then its Etherscan-compatible API, no key: `txlist`, `txlistinternal`, `tokentx`, `balance`, `tokenbalance`. Paged by start block past Blockscout's 10 000-entry window, with retries on its rate limit. Blockscout without a key answers only a few requests per half hour and IP address (about ten were seen; a sync needs at least six), then HTTP 429.
- **EVM with Alchemy** (when `pnpm setup:alchemy` has stored a key, and the wallet names no `api` endpoint of its own): see [Alchemy](#alchemy) below.

**Accounts**: one per wallet and asset, with the chain as source: _Wallet NYM ···w6d0y_, _Wallet USDC (Base) ···81efcf_ (an EVM address is the same on every EVM chain, so the chain is named). Balance and its day as for Kraken.

**Bookings**: one per movement, valued at the day's rate (CoinGecko first, Kraken as the fallback; no `prefer`, this is no exchange booking).

| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| sent / received                                  | movement `transfer`, sourceId `<hash>:m<msg>:e<event>.<n>:<asset>` (Cosmos), `<hash>:value` / `:erc20:<contract>:<from>:<to>:<value>:<n>` / `:internal:<i>` (EVM; Alchemy: `:internal:trace:<address>`), `counterpartyAddress` = the other address | needed – unless the other address is an own wallet                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| between two own wallets                          | both legs share `txRef` (the hash)                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | own transfer (1360), paired by the hash                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| to an own wallet not synced yet                  | as above                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | own transfer (1360), by the address                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| the fee this address paid (also for a failed tx) | movement `fee`, sourceId `<hash>:fee`                                                                                                                                                                                                              | network fee: the transaction is the receipt                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| a staking reward (from `distribution`)           | movement `reward`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | none (as an exchange's reward); the same suggested account (2700, or the settings')                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| a delegation (to `bonded_tokens_pool`)           | movement `stake`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | none, kind `crypto-stake`: the tokens stay ours; **not 1360** – their return after unbonding is booked apart, without a transaction, when the unbonding ends (#1029) – a transit account would balance only after the unbonding period, and not where the end's block is unknown; the account is the adviser's call |

`txRef` is the hash exactly as the chain gives it: Cosmos upper-case hex, EVM `0x` + lower-case hex. Kraken's crypto deposits and withdrawals carry the on-chain hash as `chainTxRef` (from DepositStatus/WithdrawStatus), so they pair with the wallet booking of the same hash, whatever the euro amounts. Every booking keeps `explorerUrl`, its transaction in the block explorer; _Zahlungen_ shows _Im Block-Explorer ansehen_ and the _Gegenadresse_. If Kraken refuses those lists (a key without _Funds → Query_), the bridge logs it and the Kraken card says so; bookings imported before this existed get the hash once a sync reaches them again (choose an earlier start day). Bookings keep the time where the source gives one (`bookedAt`: Kraken, own wallets, CAMT `DtTm`), shown in German time; each leg of a trade names the other (_Tausch → 128 USDC_).

**Left out, and said so**: denoms and tokens not in the chain's list (IBC vouchers `ibc/…`, factory denoms, and an ERC-20 beyond the listed USDC and, on Ethereum and Base, ALEPH that the wallet never touched – an airdrop, spam; a contract can call itself "USDC"); entries without a rate (NYX has none at CoinGecko or Kraken), fetched again next time; a pruned node's older history. The sync shows from which day the node knows the chain, found by asking for block 1, because a node restored from a snapshot says 0 in `/status`. Use an archive node then. **Not seen at all**: vesting, and on rollups (Base, Optimism, Arbitrum) the L1 data fee, which Blockscout's Etherscan-compatible API does not report. The balance shows the truth. When the booked quantities do not add up to it, the wallet card says so (_Gebucht …, Bestand …_) for a person to look at.

**Dust and lookalike addresses.** An incoming transfer worth less than a cent at the day's rate is dust: it needs no receipt (`crypto-dust`, badge _Staub_), and its amount shows as `< 0,01 EUR` rather than `0,00 EUR`. Dust is how address poisoning works: an address made to start and end like one the person uses sends a tiny amount, so it stands next to the real one in the history and gets copied as the next recipient. When the sender's address shares the first and last four characters (after `0x`, or after the bech32 prefix) with an own wallet or with the other side of any wallet booking that is not dust, the booking names that address and shows a warning (`matching/dust.js`). Two unrelated addresses share them about once in four billion.

**What the node sees**: the address and the Mac's IP address, only on _Synchronisieren_ (consent screen, _Blockchain-Abfrage_); Alchemy also ties the queries to the Alchemy account whose key it is, and when the bridge asks it for a token's Uniswap pool, it sees the token's contract and the block. The bridge's log has counts, never an address or an amount.

**Swaps on a DEX** (issue #115): in one transaction an own EVM wallet gives one asset and gets another, e.g. a token to a router and ETH back from it as an internal transfer. The booked legs become _Tausch_ (movement `trade`, kind `crypto-swap`), and the transaction in the block explorer is their receipt. The purpose names both sides and the router, e.g. "Tausch: 30.000 XYZ (nicht gelistet) → 0,143 ETH über MetaMask Swap (Router) · Gas 0,00018 ETH". The gas stays a fee entry of its own (network fee, no receipt) and names the swap it paid for. A token not in the chain's list is named by its own claim and marked. It is **booked in an account of its own** once the wallet has sent it itself or the caller names its contract (a token only ever received – an airdrop – is not), and priced by its **contract** at CoinGecko, else by its Uniswap pool (see [The rate of a day](#the-rate-of-a-day)), never by the symbol it claims. It is never booked under a symbol that is a listed asset's (ETH, USDC, USDT, DAI, WBTC, …) or another token's already. Its balance is read with the others (Alchemy `alchemy_getTokenBalances`, Blockscout `tokenbalance`), and the monthly statement gives it a section of its own, headed "XYZ (laut Token-Vertrag)" with its contract below and valued by that contract. Without a rate it waits as "ohne Kurs" and is tried again on the next sync. Two booked legs relate as _Tausch_ by their hash. Known routers and WETH are named (`packages/chains/src/registry.js` `KNOWN_EVM_CONTRACTS`); other contracts stay "Vertrag". Tax-wise a swap is a disposal and an acquisition at the day's value, for the adviser.

**Across chains** (issue #98): each side has its own transaction and hash, so the shared hash does not pair them.

- An **IBC transfer** names its receiver on the other chain. The receiver is looked up among the own wallets of that chain, found by the address's bech32 prefix. An own receiver makes the transfer an own transfer (1360). The tokens usually arrive there as an `ibc/…` voucher, which is not booked.
- A **bridge** between own EVM wallets is paired only when all of this holds:
  - the arrival is an internal transfer (from the bridge contract);
  - the sending is on another chain and of the same asset;
  - the arrival comes 0 to 8 days after the sending (an L2 → L1 withdrawal takes 7);
  - the received quantity is at most the sent one and at least 97 % of it;
  - each side has only the other.

  Otherwise it stays a question. _Keine Umbuchung_ keeps a pair apart. The pair shows under _Gehört zusammen mit_ as _Umbuchung · über Bridge_.

**By hand**, for everything the rules miss (another bridge, an account not synced, an exchange's payout to the bank): _Kein fremder Beleg … → Als Gegenbuchung verknüpfen …_ in the payment lists bookings on other accounts, the other way, within a month, the closest amount first, with a search. The pair is kept sealed in the matching settings (`ownTransfers`) and holds through every run: an own transfer (1360), _Umbuchung · von dir verknüpft_ on both sides. _Verknüpfung lösen_ undoes it and keeps the pair apart.

A network fee shares the hash of its booking and shows as _Gebühr dazu_. That explains the fee, not where the booking's coins came from or went.

### Alchemy

With an Alchemy API key (`pnpm setup:alchemy`: hidden prompt, `eth_chainId` on every network as a check, macOS keychain, service `belege-bridge`, account `alchemy`; Enter keeps it, `-` deletes it) the bridge reads EVM wallets from `https://<network>.g.alchemy.com/v2/<key>` – `eth-mainnet`, `base-mainnet`, `arb-mainnet`, `opt-mainnet`, `polygon-mainnet` – instead of Blockscout. The key is read from the keychain on every sync (no restart), goes only into the URL of the requests to Alchemy and never into a log line, an error, an answer or the app; `GET /chains` says `alchemy: true` or `false`. A wallet with an `api` endpoint of its own is read there, never through Alchemy. _Integrationen → Eigene Wallets_ says which source is used, and without a key how to set one up.

What is asked, JSON-RPC, several calls as one batch (at most 50):

1. `eth_chainId` – the endpoint must serve the chain (`WALLET_WRONG_CHAIN` otherwise; nothing else is asked).
2. `alchemy_getAssetTransfers`, once with `fromAddress`, once with `toAddress` (both in one call would mean "from and to"): categories `external`, `erc20` and `internal` where Alchemy offers it (Ethereum, Base, Polygon), `withMetadata: true` (the block time), `excludeZeroValue: true`, `order: asc`, `maxCount: 0x3e8`, then `pageKey` (valid 10 minutes).
3. For every transaction a transfer names the address as its sender: `eth_getTransactionByHash` (who really sent it – a token can leave by `transferFrom` in someone else's transaction, which costs us no gas) and `eth_getTransactionReceipt` (the gas: `gasUsed × effectiveGasPrice`; `status`).
4. Transactions the address sent that moved nothing – a failed one, an approval, a claim that only brought something in – are in no transfer list. The nonce counts each of them: `eth_blockNumber` and `eth_getTransactionCount` at the head; where the count rises by more than the known transactions explain, the block range is cut into 16 parts and the count asked at every cut (one batch), until single blocks are left; `eth_getBlockByNumber` with transactions finds them, `eth_getTransactionReceipt` gives their gas. At most 300 such blocks per sync (`WALLET_TOO_MANY` beyond).
5. For value received from someone else's transaction: `eth_getTransactionReceipt` (batched), and a reverted one books nothing. Alchemy was not seen to list reverted transactions, but does not say it never does.
6. A receipt without `status` (before Byzantium, Ethereum block 4 370 000, October 2017) does not say whether the transaction went through; Blockscout knows it from its traces. Through Alchemy its gas is booked (paid either way), its value is not, and the result counts it (`unknownStatus`, in the bridge's log as _without a receipt status_). For such old history, sync through Blockscout (an own `api` endpoint).
7. `eth_getBalance`, and `alchemy_getTokenBalances` for the listed contracts and the unlisted ones that are booked (100 contracts a call); a token never touched is not asked for.

**Compute units.** Alchemy charges every call its compute units – in a batch each call on its own: `eth_chainId` 0, `eth_blockNumber` 10, `alchemy_getAssetTransfers` 120, the others here 20 – and a free app gets 300 a second over a 10-second token bucket (Alchemy's docs, _Compute unit costs_ and _Throughput_). A batch of 50 receipts is 1000 at once, so the bridge spends at most 250 a second, up to four seconds ahead, and waits before a call that would spend more; a batch is also cut so it never costs more than that. What Alchemy still refuses with 429 (the key's budget is shared with anything else using it) is sent again, only the refused calls, after 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and 16 seconds; then `WALLET_ALCHEMY_RATE_LIMIT`. A wallet with many transactions therefore takes a while: about a second for every six it sent.

Symbols and decimals come from the registry, never from Alchemy's `asset` or `rawContract.decimal` (which is what the token says about itself).

**Ids, and changing the source.** Alchemy's answers are turned into Blockscout's lists and then into entries by the same code, so the value (`<hash>:value`), the gas (`<hash>:fee`) and a token transfer (`<hash>:erc20:<contract>:<from>:<to>:<value>:<n>`: Blockscout's `tokentx` gives no log index, so neither side uses Alchemy's) get the same id from both – a wallet synced through Blockscout and then through Alchemy, or the other way round, books nothing twice (tested on one made-up chain read through both fakes). An internal transfer cannot: Blockscout numbers it by its place among all of the transaction's calls (`index`), Alchemy by its trace address among those that moved value (`uniqueId` `<hash>:internal:<trace>`), and neither can be computed from the other. Alchemy's gets an id of its own, `<hash>:internal:trace:<trace>`, which can never equal a Blockscout one; the app then pairs an incoming entry whose id is not stored with a stored booking of the same transaction, kind (gas, value, token, internal), quantity and other address that no incoming entry names, each stored one once, and keeps the stored id (`reconcileSourceIds` in `app/src/lib/wallets/wallet-sync.js`).

**Errors**: 401 → `WALLET_ALCHEMY_AUTH` (run `pnpm setup:alchemy` again); 403 → `WALLET_ALCHEMY_DENIED` (network not enabled for the Alchemy app, capacity used up, app inactive; the message quotes Alchemy's words without links); HTTP 429 or an item with error code 429 inside a batch (a batch always answers 200) → retried up to six times as above, then 429 `WALLET_ALCHEMY_RATE_LIMIT`; any other refusal → `WALLET_ALCHEMY` with Alchemy's code.

**Gaps**: Alchemy has no `internal` category on Arbitrum and Optimism; there the bridge still reads `txlistinternal` from Blockscout (with its `eth_chainId` check: two requests per sync instead of six or more). The L1 data fee of rollups is still left out – the receipts of Base and Optimism name it (`l1Fee`), but taking it would change the amount of fees already booked from Blockscout; a later change. A transaction that the nonce shows but whose block has no transaction from the address (a smart-contract wallet, whose nonce counts contract creations) is skipped.

**Checked** (2026-09-26): the five endpoint hosts, the parameters, categories, the `internal` networks, the 1000 page limit, the batch limit and the error shapes (401 `-32600` _Must be authenticated!_, 403, 429 code 429, `-32601` _Unsupported method_, `-32602` _invalid … argument_) against Alchemy's documentation; that every host answers, and that a made-up key gets exactly that 401 body. **By real calls** on Ethereum mainnet (with a real key, for a public burn address only): `alchemy_getAssetTransfers` including a second page by `pageKey`, `rawContract.value` and `blockNum` as hex, `metadata.blockTimestamp` as ISO, `uniqueId` as `<hash>:external`, `<hash>:log:<n>` and `<hash>:internal:<trace>` with underscores (`3_0`, `0_3_0`; 2000 ids, all unique), receipts and transactions in hex, a batch answered as an array, `eth_getTransactionCount` at an old block, `alchemy_getTokenBalances` with 66-character padded balances. The fake (`bridge/test/support/fake-alchemy.js`) answers in these shapes and is as strict as the documented API about method names, parameter count and types (a string `"true"` where a boolean belongs is refused). **Not seen**: a reverted transaction in the transfer lists (about 400 looked at), and a pre-Byzantium receipt through Alchemy – both are handled as above anyway.

### Bitcoin

A Bitcoin wallet is read by its account's **extended public key**, not by one address: a wallet spreads its coins over many addresses and sends change to new ones. Belege takes a **zpub** (native SegWit, `bc1q…`), a **ypub** (`3…`) or an **xpub**. Wallets hand out an xpub for more than one kind of address, so `pnpm setup:bitcoin` asks for the xpub's kind: `bc1q…` or `1…`.

- **The key stays in the bridge.** `pnpm setup:bitcoin` puts it into the macOS keychain or the Windows Credential Manager (account `bitcoin`, JSON `{ key, type }`); `BITCOIN_XPUB` from `.env` is offered once. The app knows the wallet only by a fingerprint of the key (`btc-` + 8 hex): _Integrationen → Eigene Wallets → Bitcoin → Schlüssel aus der Bridge übernehmen_. On every sync the bridge checks the fingerprint, so a replaced key does not book onto the old account (`WALLET_KEY_CHANGED`).
- **Addresses** are derived in the bridge (`/0/i` receive, `/1/i` change) until 20 in a row are unused. Each is asked of an Esplora API: `mempool.space/api` by default, `blockstream.info/api` as an alternative, or your own. That API sees all these addresses from one IP and can tell they belong together (consent screen, _Blockchain-Abfrage_); an own Esplora avoids it.
- **Bookings**, from the wallet's point of view (all its addresses together), confirmed transactions only:
  - all inputs ours: sent. The fee is a booking of its own (`<txid>:fee`), the rest went to others (`<txid>:value`). Change to our own addresses is no booking; a transaction only between our addresses is just its fee.
  - no input ours: received what went to our addresses.
  - some inputs ours (payjoin, coinjoin): one booking with the net change, fee included, and a note.
- `txRef` is the txid. A Kraken BTC deposit or withdrawal carries the same txid as `chainTxRef`, so the two pair as an own transfer.
- One account, _Wallet BTC ···<fingerprint>_, with its balance (confirmed, from the Esplora API).

### Filecoin

An own Filecoin wallet is added by its address: f1 (secp256k1), f3 (BLS) or f410f (delegated – also typed in its Ethereum form `0x…`, which is the same account and is kept as f410f), each checked by its blake2b checksum before anything is asked, or f0 (an ID). The bridge reads it from Filfox's public API, without a key: `address/<a>` for the balance and `address/<a>/transfers` for every movement, 100 a page (`packages/chains/src/filecoin.js`). Per message, each send and each receipt becomes a booking with the other side's address; its miner fee and burn fee together one fee booking. The message CID (`bafy2bzace…`) is the hash – the same one Kraken reports for a deposit, so a withdrawal from the wallet and the deposit on Kraken pair as an own transfer, and the deposit's detail names Filecoin. Filfox sees the address and this Mac's IP address; an address with more than 20 000 movements is refused as too large.

**Tokens on the FEVM and swaps (issue #301).** Where the address has token transfers, the bridge also reads `address/<a>/token-transfers`. Only listed tokens are booked, by contract: **USDFC** (`0x80b98d3aa09ffff255c3ba4a241111ff1262f045`, Secured Finance's FIL-backed stablecoin; checked against Secured Finance's contract list, CoinGecko and the contract itself). A token that calls itself USDFC from any other contract is counted, never booked. A token's balance is the sum of its whole history.

A message in which the wallet gives one asset and gets another is a **swap**, as on the EVM chains: FIL for USDFC, or USDFC for FIL (the FIL comes back in the same message). Both legs are booked as a trade (_Tausch_) and need no receipt; the gas stays a fee of its own and names the swap. The router is named from the message's receiver (`message/<cid>`, asked for swaps only) where it is known from Sushi's own config: _SushiSwap_ (the router its app uses), _SushiSwap V2 (Router)_, _WFIL_. USDFC's euro rate comes from CoinGecko (`usdfc`), else from the swap's other side; it is never taken as 1 USD without a source. In the crypto-ledger export USDFC is `eip155:314/erc20:0x80b9…f045`.

**Paying into Filecoin Pay.** USDFC a wallet sends to the Filecoin Pay contract (Filecoin Onchain Cloud; mainnet `0x23b1e018F08BB982348b15a86ee926eEBf7F4DAa`, `f410feoy…l36cyi`, from `@filoz/synapse-core`'s published addresses) goes into the payer's own balance in that contract: it stays the payer's, and can be taken out again, until storage is settled from it. Belege knows the contract by its address (`app/src/lib/wallets/known-contracts.js`), names the payment "Filecoin Pay" and books it as an own transfer without a receipt, on 1360 by default – the same for money that comes back from it. What the contract pays storage providers out of that balance is not a movement of the wallet: that expense has a statement of its own (below). The account is a setting (Einstellungen → Buchhaltung, "Konto für Guthaben in Zahlungsverträgen"; 1360 unless another is entered). An address a person named themselves ("Was war diese Zahlung?") keeps what they said.

**The Filecoin Pay usage statement.** Integrationen → Eigene Wallets → _Nachweise_ shows a card "Filecoin Pay" once the books keep a Filecoin wallet a contract can hold a balance for (`f410f…`). "Filecoin-Wallets prüfen" asks for each such wallet whether it has a balance or payment rails in the contract; nothing is asked before. For a wallet that has, a month can be shown – opening balance, paid in, taken out, settled, closing balance, and each settlement with its rail, provider, the day it paid up to, the USDFC and their euro value – and made an Eigenbeleg ("Verbrauchsnachweis erstellen"): a PDF with the same lines, how each settlement was split (provider, commission, network fee) with its transaction, the rails with their providers' addresses, the source and the limits. It is a receipt of its own number range (`EB-YYYY-NNN`, `selfReceipt.kind` `filecoin-pay-statement`, the numbers kept in `selfReceipt.filecoinPay`), once per payer and month, and is not linked to a booking: the expense is in no wallet.

The bridge reads it (`filecoinpay.statement`, `@le-space/chains/filecoin-pay`): settlements and the balance from the index Filecoin Pay's makers keep (a public subgraph at `api.goldsky.com`), what was paid in and taken out from the contract's events at Blockscout (`filecoin.blockscout.com`). When the index does not answer, everything comes from Blockscout's events; when Blockscout does not, paid in and taken out come from Filfox's token transfers – then what another address paid into the balance is missing, and the statement says so. Each of them sees the payer's address and the bridge's IP address. Blockscout without a key answers about ten requests and then none for minutes; with a Blockscout API key (`pnpm setup:blockscout`: hidden prompt, one test call, macOS keychain, service `belege-bridge`, account `blockscout`; Enter keeps it, `-` deletes it) the bridge asks `api.blockscout.com` instead, the key in the `authorization` header and in no URL.

**Limits**: only USDFC is read. A settlement pays a rail up to the moment it names and happens when someone asks for it, so a month's settlements need not cover exactly that month, and what has run up since the last one is not in the statement. What a payer itself earns over rails is not read; its balances would be off by it.

### NEAR

An own NEAR account is added by its account id: a named one (`name.near`) or an implicit one (64 hex characters), kept in lower case. The bridge reads it without a key (`packages/chains/src/near-wallet.js`, on the reader that came from Eigenbeleg, `near.js`):

- **NearBlocks** (`api.nearblocks.io`, v3): `accounts/<id>/txns` for the transactions the account signed or received, `accounts/<id>/ft-txns` for its NEP-141 token movements, 25 a page, the whole history (more than 5 000 of either is refused, `WALLET_TOO_LARGE`).
- **An archival RPC** (`archival-rpc.mainnet.fastnear.com`): `view_account` for the balance now (liquid plus what a validator holds locked), `ft_balance_of` for a listed token, and `EXPERIMENTAL_tx_status` for what a storage deposit kept.

Both see the account id and this Mac's IP address. Own endpoints can be named for either (`api`, `rpc`).

Per transaction: the NEAR it moved a booking sent or received with the other account, and the gas of a transaction the account signed a fee of its own (`<hash>:fee`); a failed transaction costs its fee only. The 1 yoctoNEAR a token transfer (or an unwrap) attaches is no payment, but it leaves the account for the token contract to keep: a network fee of its own under `<hash>:deposit` ("Security deposit of the token transfer"), so that the bookings add up to the balance to the last yoctoNEAR (#1028); a failed transfer gives it back and books none. of a storage deposit only what the contract kept is booked. A storage deposit the account sent at most five minutes before a swap, to a contract the swap involves (a token given or got, wNEAR counting as NEAR, or the DEX that took it), is part of that swap (#968): a fee of the swap like its gas, with its own transaction's gas, named under the swap (`partOf`), and the swap's text names what such deposits kept. A refund (`storage_withdraw`, `storage_unregister`) never is. What else a deposit was for is read from the chain too: its call's arguments (`account_id`, `registration_only`, a DEX's token list) come with the answer the RPC gives for what the contract kept, so no request is added. A deposit that registered another account at a token, followed within five minutes by a transfer of that token to exactly that account, is a cost of that transfer (`enables`); one that registered the wallet itself before it first received the token, within a day, a cost of that receipt; one at a token a swap's sides do not name, five minutes before a swap whose route passed through that token (an aggregator's – read from the swap's own receipts, one RPC request per such swap), part of that swap. Each is a network fee shown with what it was for, without a receipt of its own, its own transaction's gas with it; a deposit for a transfer takes the account of the transfer's gas. Anything else is a storage deposit of its own: a network fee too, "Kein Beleg nötig" with its reason – NEAR a contract keeps for an account's storage is no payment for a service. A booking an earlier sync made of such a deposit (before, a payment waiting for its receipt) is changed in place on the next sync; the reader's rules are in `packages/chains/src/near-storage.js`. The hash is the transaction's (base58), the one Kraken names for a withdrawal – so a withdrawal on Kraken and what arrives on the account pair as an own transfer, and the withdrawal's detail names NEAR and links NearBlocks.

**Booked**: NEAR and **USDC** by Circle's own contract (`17208628…36133a1`). Wrapped NEAR (wNEAR, the token of `wrap.near`) is booked as the NEAR it is: wrapping and unwrapping are movements inside the wallet and leave only their gas, the balance is the account's NEAR plus its wNEAR, and wNEAR given to a DEX that pays out USDC is one swap of NEAR for USDC, named after the DEX. Any other token – the bridged USDC.e, USDT, and what was airdropped – is counted and left out: a token's symbol is its own claim. In the crypto-ledger export NEAR is `near:mainnet/slip44:397`, USDC `near:mainnet/nep141:<contract>`.

**Not read yet**: NEAR that reaches the account inside another transaction's inner receipts – a staking pool paying out a withdrawal, say. The balance comes from the RPC, not from the bookings, so such a gap shows as a difference.

NearBlocks limits requests without a key; the reader waits and asks again (up to about half a minute per request). A NearBlocks key in the bridge's keychain is not built yet.

### Monero

Monero hides amounts, senders and receivers on its chain, so nothing can be read by an address. Instead Belege imports the history your wallet exports, and reads it only in the browser. No view key and no file leaves the computer.

1. _Integrationen → Eigene Wallets_: add a wallet, chain Monero, your primary address (`4…`) or a subaddress (`8…`). The address only names the account.
2. Export the history from the wallet:
   - **Monero GUI:** _History_ → _Export_ (CSV);
   - **CLI:** `export_transfers all output=monero.csv`.
3. "Verlauf importieren (CSV)" on the wallet reads the file:
   - confirmed transfers only (in, out, a mined block); pool, pending and failed ones are left out;
   - each transfer is valued at the day's rate (CoinGecko, `monero`);
   - an outgoing transfer's fee is booked apart, as for every wallet; an incoming one's was paid by the sender.

   Importing the same or a longer file again adds only what is new. The account's balance and its balances at each month's start and end: from the CLI export, the wallet's own running balance – a figure the wallet worked out, so the monthly statement reconciles against it (#287); the GUI export has none, so there they are what the file adds up to.

Both formats were taken from the wallets' own source (monero-gui `TransactionHistory::writeCSV`, monero `export_transfers`). The GUI's date column follows the language, so its `epoch` column is used; the CLI's timestamps are UTC.

**XMR from private holdings** (a contribution) is a received transfer like any other. It needs no third-party receipt: mark it "Kein fremder Beleg … / Kein Beleg nötig" with a reason, and take over the account your tax adviser names (sole proprietors: 1890 Privateinlagen; a UG or GmbH books it against the shareholder). A **customer's payment** in XMR is matched against our invoice like a bank payment, at the day's euro value.

## Aleph Cloud credits (issue #113)

Aleph issues no invoice: hosting and storage are paid in credits. Under Integrationen → Aleph Cloud, the own EVM wallets (when "Eigene Ethereum-Adressen bei Aleph prüfen" is on) and any Aleph account typed in are asked at Aleph's public API, read only (`POST /aleph/accounts`, `packages/chains/src/aleph.js`). For an account and a month, "Verbrauchsnachweis erstellen" makes an Eigenbeleg (`app/src/lib/aleph/`):

- **Balances:** opening and closing balance are the balance now, less the net of `credit_history/summary` since the month began or ended. Opening + in − out = closing is checked and printed.
- **Lines:** top-ups (purchases with their price and transaction, transfers in), transfers out, and consumption per UTC day. Storage is one line, since Aleph bills all stores together with their number and size; each instance or program gets its own line, named from its message.
- **Euro value:** the per-credit USD price of the last purchase up to that day (without one, Aleph's list price of 1 USD per 1,000,000 credits), times the ECB rate of the day. The tax adviser has to confirm this valuation.
- **Stored:** as a receipt in the EB range, source `eigenbeleg`, `sourceRef aleph:<address>:<month>`, not linked to a booking. The numbers stay on the receipt (`selfReceipt.aleph`) for a later invoice through the invoice app.

A month in which nothing moved makes none. Checked on 2026-09-27 against two own accounts for two months: the balances agreed to the credit.

**The payment behind a purchase.** For every purchase Aleph names the transaction that paid it. "Bei Aleph prüfen" reads the purchases of each account found (from the running month's statement, which lists every purchase up to now) and keeps account, hash, time and credits in the sealed settings; making a statement does the same. A booking that left the books with that hash – on an own wallet, or an exchange's withdrawal by its on-chain hash – is then explained by itself: a purchase of Aleph credits, no receipt of its own, since the account's monthly statement is the document. Its account is suggested as **4960** Mieten für Einrichtungen, or the one set under _Einstellungen → Buchhaltung (DATEV)_ – for the tax adviser to confirm; another account confirmed on such a payment becomes the setting. The network fee of the same transaction stays a fee. A bridge from before this lists only the purchases of the month asked.

## In the export

The Buchungstext of a crypto booking ends with what moved, e.g. `Konto B -0,015 BTC`. The name is shortened when the 60 characters DATEV allows are not enough; the quantity never is. The amount (Umsatz) is in EUR, like every other line.

Each own wallet can have a **name** (a project, a purpose), a **ledger account per asset** and a **cost centre**, set when it is added or later under _Bezeichnung, Konto, Kostenstelle_. The name replaces the address tail on all its asset accounts (`Projekt Nord · USDC (Ethereum)`); the cost centre goes to all of them, into the DATEV export as _KOST1_.

**Every asset its own ledger account.** A DATEV account sums in euros: with NEAR and USDC on one number, holdings and gains per asset can no longer be told apart. So the dialog has one field per asset account (`Konto (Finanzkonto) für USDC`); a change there, or in a row of _Einstellungen → Buchhaltung (DATEV)_, sets that asset's number only. The wallet keeps them per asset in the sealed settings (`ledgerAccounts: { NEAR: '…', USDC: '…' }`). A sync never overwrites a number an asset account has; one without gets the wallet's number for its asset. When a wallet is added, its number is the chain's own asset's (NEAR, ETH, AKT …); every other asset gets its own afterwards, with a suggestion in the settings: the chart account that names the wallet and the asset ("Wallet Projekt Nord USDC"), else the next free number after the other wallets' – never one another asset uses.

Wallets from before kept one number for all their assets (`ledgerAccount`), and a sync gave it to every asset account. Their accounts keep what they have; nothing is renumbered. _Einstellungen → Buchhaltung (DATEV)_ flags every ledger account that holds two different assets ("ETH und USDC teilen 1210 – eigenes Konto für USDC eintragen"), with a number for the asset that should move; it changes only when it is taken and saved. The first per-asset number set drops the wallet's old one number, so that an older device, which still gave that number to every asset, no longer overwrites them. Exchange accounts (Kraken) were one account per asset with a number of their own already; the same check covers them.

The export reads each account's ledger account when it runs; bookings do not store it. So months not yet exported go out with the new number. Months your tax adviser already has were booked on the old shared account: there they need a reposting (Umbuchung) from the old account to the new one, or a new export of those months.

## In the app

The detail of a payment shows **Menge** (`-0,015 BTC`) and **Kurs** (`60.000,00 EUR je BTC · CoinGecko, 01.09.2026 00:00 UTC`).

**Who sent, who received.** A crypto booking always names both sides, `Von … → An …`, in the list and in the detail. Each side is named by the first of these that applies:

- an own wallet, by its name, marked _eigene_;
- a partner known by the address;
- a module the reader names (staking, IBC, fee collector);
- else the short address, marked _fremde Adresse_, with the full address on hover.

An IBC receiver is looked up on its own chain. A network fee goes from the own wallet to _Netzwerk (Gebühr)_. The list's title is the other side's name, never a full hex address (`bank/payee.js`).

**Finding the mail of a crypto payment.** _Im privaten Postfach suchen_ on an own wallet's booking searches by what a vendor's confirmation names: the transaction hash, the other address and the quantity (exact and to two decimals, with a point and a comma), ± 3 days; as text the vendor learned for that address, else the memo's first word. Not the euro amount: that is our own valuation. A receipt linked to a wallet payment teaches its address (`addr:<chain or evm>:<address>` among the partner's aliases), so the next payment to the same address scores the vendor and searches its mail domains. An incoming transfer with a memo and nothing else explaining it says that it is usually one's own withdrawal from an exchange, with the memo typed there.

**Which receipts a crypto payment gets.** For an own wallet's booking, a receipt or mail is a candidate – in the suggestions, the KI-Vorschlag and the automatic matching – only when its subject, mail text, read text, file name or number names the payment: the transaction hash, the other address, or the quantity together with the asset's symbol (at least three significant digits; `cryptoEvidence` in `matching/view.js`). A close euro amount is no sign: crypto is valued at the day's rate. Any receipt can still be linked by hand under _Alle Belege_. A link in a mail is shortened to its host before the text goes to the model (`[LINK host]`), so a hash only in an explorer link is found in the mail's own text, not in a PDF.
